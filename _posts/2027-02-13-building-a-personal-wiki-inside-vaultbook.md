A wiki is usually described as a kind of software, and the description is misleading. What people actually mean by the word is a condition: a body of knowledge dense enough and connected enough that asking it a question produces an answer assembled from several places at once. The tooling is incidental. The condition is what matters, and the condition can arrive without anybody having installed anything designed to produce it.

Most people who want one build it deliberately. They choose a tool intended for the purpose, decide on a structure, and then face the difficulty that defeats the majority of such attempts, which is that a wiki with four pages is not a wiki. It is four pages. The value arrives only after a substantial accumulation, and the accumulation requires months of effort during which the thing provides almost nothing in return.

![Building a Personal Wiki Inside VaultBook](/assets/img/feature-screenshot-placeholder.svg)

That gap between effort and reward is why so many deliberately constructed personal wikis are abandoned. Somebody spends a fortnight designing a structure, populates it enthusiastically for a month, and then encounters a busy period during which maintaining something that has not yet begun to pay is the obvious thing to drop. The tool was capable throughout. The problem was the order in which the costs and benefits arrived.
<!--more-->


There is another route to the same condition, and it inverts that order entirely. Somebody who takes notes because they need the notes, structures them because structure helps them personally, and labels them because labels help them find things is doing work that pays immediately. Each individual entry justifies itself on the day it is written. Nothing is being built for a future state, which means nothing is being maintained on faith.

What happens after enough of that accumulates is the interesting part. Connections that nobody created begin to exist, because two entries carrying the same description are connected whether or not anybody linked them. A query that would have needed to be a manual trace across several documents becomes a single operation. The collection starts answering questions its owner did not think to ask when writing any of the individual pieces.

The threshold is real and identifiable. Below a certain volume, retrieval returns the thing somebody was looking for, which is useful and unremarkable. Above it, retrieval begins returning a small constellation of related material from different parts of the collection, and the person reading it learns something about their own subject rather than merely locating a fact. The transition is not gradual so much as noticed suddenly, usually while looking for something else.

None of this requires explicit linking, which is worth stating because explicit linking is what most people think a wiki is made of. A link is a statement that two things are related, created by hand, maintained by hand, and complete only to the extent that somebody remembered. A shared description is the same statement made once and applied to everything carrying it, including items added afterward. The second scales in a way the first cannot.

What follows is a conversation between two people who each built one without intending to, in fields with nothing in common, and who identified the same threshold at almost the same point.


## Two collections that grew into something else

Raj had proposed it after something Priya wrote in the group.

‘You said a thing about searching for a side effect you had never studied,’ he said.

‘Which I have been thinking about since.’ Priya was matter of fact. ‘Because it was not a search result. It was a briefing.’

‘Say what happened.’

‘A collaborator asked me about a toxicity effect I have never worked on directly.’ She counted. ‘Which I would have answered by saying I did not know, and instead I searched it out of curiosity while she was still talking.’

‘And?’

‘And it appeared in a paper note from about eleven months ago, in an experiment log where I had recorded something anomalous, and in feedback from my supervisor that I had entirely forgotten.’

‘Three places you did not connect.’

‘Three places I did not connect, and reading them together in twenty seconds, I understood something I did not know I understood.’ Priya was precise. ‘Which is a strange sentence and it is accurate.’

‘Say why you called me.’

‘Because I suspect the same thing has happened to you and I want to know whether it feels the same from inside a completely different subject.’

Raj laughed.

‘It has happened and I have not had language for it, and I want to describe mine before you tell me what you think it is.’

‘Go.’

‘I have been building something on mezcal economics for about eight months and I never decided to.’ He counted. ‘Research notes about regulation. Interview transcripts. Production notes. Archived articles. Footage records.’

‘Which are different kinds of thing.’

‘Which are completely different kinds of thing in completely different parts of my structure, and they behave as one body of material when I ask about the subject.’ He shrugged. ‘Which I had assumed was a search working well rather than anything more interesting.’

‘It is both.’

‘Say what you mean.’

‘That the search is doing exactly what a search does, and what makes it feel like something else is that there is enough there for it to find.’


## Tracing one technique

‘Show me yours properly,’ Raj said. ‘Because I want to see the density.’

Priya shared and typed a technique name.

‘That is one technique,’ she said. ‘A delivery method I have worked with for about two years.’

‘Say the results.’

‘Twelve paper entries where it is a category rather than a mention.’ She scrolled. ‘Three experiment entries where it appears in a section. Two supervision records where it was discussed. And the chapter where I analyse it.’

‘Eighteen things.’

‘Eighteen things across four different parts of my structure, which took four seconds, and which would have been an afternoon of remembering three years ago.’

‘Say the ordering.’

‘The ones carrying it as a category come first, comfortably.’ Priya was precise. ‘Which is correct, because a category is a claim that the entry is about the technique, and a body mention is an occurrence.’

‘So a paper that mentions it four times in passing.’

‘Sits below a paper I labelled with it, which is exactly right, because the second is about the technique and the first is about something else.’ She shrugged. ‘And the chapter comes near the top because the title carries it, which is the strongest signal there is.’

Raj asked about the experiment entries.

‘Say why those matter.’

‘Because they are the ones nobody would find by any other method.’ Priya was definite. ‘An experiment record is titled with a date and a subject. The technique is in a methods section inside it.’

‘Which is buried.’

‘Which is buried three levels down and is reachable, and it is where the actual practical knowledge sits.’ She counted. ‘A paper tells me what somebody claimed. My own experiment log tells me what happened when I did it, including the part that went wrong.’

‘Which is the more useful thing.’

‘Which is enormously more useful and is the least findable by any conventional method, and that asymmetry is the whole argument.’

‘Say what you do with eighteen results.’

‘I read the top six, which is one page, and that is generally sufficient.’ Priya was matter of fact. ‘The paginated set means I am not scrolling through everything. I look at the strongest and go further only if the answer is not there.’

‘How often is it not there?’

‘Perhaps one time in six, and the second page is nearly always enough.’

She mentioned the suggestions.

‘There is a second layer and it is the one I did not expect.’

‘Say it.’

‘When I open one of those entries, it proposes others based on similarity rather than on anything I recorded.’ She pointed. ‘Which is a different question. The search finds what matches. This finds what resembles.’

‘Give me a case.’

‘It proposed a paper about a completely different delivery approach, which I had filed under a different area and which turns out to have solved the same problem I have been struggling with.’ Priya was definite. ‘Nothing about my labelling connected those two. Nothing about my structure connected them. The similarity did.’

‘Which is the thing you could not have built.’

‘Which is the thing I could not have built and would not have thought to build, because connecting it required noticing a resemblance I had not noticed.’


## Tracing one theme

‘Now yours,’ Priya said.

Raj shared and typed.

‘Economics,’ he said. ‘Which for this project is a theme rather than a subject, and I want to show you what comes back because it is genuinely heterogeneous.’

‘Say what you have.’

‘Regulation entries, which are research about the denomination rules and what they require.’ He counted through the results. ‘Two interview records where distillers talked about margins. Production notes carrying the inline marker for economics. Footage records for the marketplace and the distillery. And an archived article about export trends that I saved months ago.’

‘Which are five kinds of material.’

‘Five kinds of material that live in five parts of my structure and that I have never thought of as one thing.’ Raj shrugged. ‘Until you ask about the subject, at which point they are one thing.’

‘Say the interview transcripts.’

‘Which are the ones that matter and which I had almost forgotten contained anything about money.’ He was matter of fact. ‘Because an interview is filed by the person, and the economics is four minutes inside a fifty-minute conversation.’

‘So the file is about a person.’

‘The file is about a person and the material inside it is about eleven different things, and the only way to reach the fourth of those eleven is to search inside it.’ Raj was definite. ‘Which is what happens, because the transcript is attached and its contents are reachable.’

‘Which is deep indexing.’

‘Which is why my documents are attached rather than sitting in a folder, and it is the single decision that makes any of this work.’

Priya asked about the markers.

‘Say what the inline ones do that categories do not.’

‘They drive the board.’ Raj showed it. ‘So my production pipeline has a column for whatever stage things are in, and the cards carry the theme.’

‘Which means?’

‘Which means I can see that my economics material is concentrated in one stage.’ He was thinking. ‘Which right now shows me that I have a great deal of it in research and almost nothing in edited sequences.’

‘Which is a gap.’

‘Which is a finding about the film rather than about my notes, and it is visible because the theme markers and the pipeline are the same board.’ Raj shrugged. ‘I would not have noticed that by looking at either separately.’

‘Say the archived article.’

‘Which appeared in the results alongside my own material and which is the thing I would emphasise.’ He was definite. ‘Because it is not mine. It is something somebody else published that I took a copy of.’

‘And it participates.’

‘It participates exactly like everything else, which means my reference on this subject includes external material and my own material in one body.’ Raj paused. ‘Which is what a reference on a subject actually is, and I built it by saving things I found interesting.’


## Nobody built this

‘Now the part I actually want to discuss,’ Priya said. ‘Because neither of us set out to make what we have just demonstrated.’

‘Which is the strange part.’

‘Say what you set out to do.’

‘Keep track of a documentary.’ Raj was matter of fact. ‘Which is a production problem. Where things are, what has been shot, what needs doing.’

‘And mine was not losing papers.’

‘Which is a filing problem.’

‘Which is a filing problem and neither of those has anything to do with what we have just shown each other.’ Priya was precise. ‘We were solving small immediate problems and something larger appeared.’

‘Say what produced it.’

‘Three things and I have thought about this.’ She counted. ‘Consistent structure, so that similar things are shaped similarly. Deliberate categories, so that the same subject is described the same way. And a search that reaches everything, including inside attached documents.’

‘Which are all things we did for immediate reasons.’

‘Every one of which paid on the day I did it.’ Priya was emphatic. ‘I use consistent sections because it helps me read my own experiment records. I label carefully because it helps me filter. Neither of those was an investment.’

‘Which is why we did not abandon it.’

‘Which is exactly why, and I think it is the whole difference between this and every deliberate attempt I have seen anybody make.’ She was definite. ‘A deliberately built one costs months before it pays anything.’

‘So people stop.’

‘People stop during the first busy fortnight, entirely reasonably, because they are maintaining something that has given them nothing yet.’ Priya shrugged. ‘Whereas this cost nothing extra at any point.’

Raj asked about the threshold.

‘When did yours start behaving differently?’

‘I have thought about this and I cannot give you a date, and I can give you a number.’ Priya was careful. ‘Somewhere between two and three hundred well-structured entries.’

‘Say what changed.’

‘Below that, a search gave me the thing I was looking for.’ She counted. ‘Above it, a search started giving me the thing I was looking for and three others that were relevant.’

‘Which is a different experience.’

‘Which is a categorically different experience and it happened without any announcement.’ Priya paused. ‘I noticed it about four months after it must have started.’

‘Mine is similar and I would put it slightly higher.’

‘Say why.’

‘Because my material is more varied.’ Raj was thinking. ‘Yours is papers, experiments, and chapters, which are three kinds of thing. Mine is research, transcripts, footage records, production notes, and archived articles, which is five.’

‘So density arrives later.’

‘So each subject needs more entries before it is covered from several directions, and covered from several directions is what produces the effect.’ He shrugged. ‘Which is a guess and it fits what I experienced.’

Priya said something she had been considering.

‘There is a version of this that does not work and I want to name it.’

‘Say it.’

‘Three hundred entries with inconsistent labels and no structure is three hundred entries.’ She was definite. ‘The volume is necessary and it is not sufficient.’

‘So the discipline matters.’

‘The discipline is the whole of it and the volume is the multiplier, and somebody accumulating carelessly for two years has a large pile rather than a reference.’


## Connections nobody made

‘I want to talk about how the connections actually work,’ Raj said, ‘because I have been describing this to somebody who uses a conventional arrangement and I could not explain it.’

‘Say what they use.’

‘Explicit links between pages, which is what everybody thinks a reference system is made of.’

‘And what is the difference?’

‘That a link is a statement somebody made once, by hand, at a moment when they remembered to.’ Priya was precise. ‘Which means the connections are exactly as complete as their author's diligence.’

‘Whereas a shared description.’

‘Whereas a shared description is a statement made once and applied to everything carrying it, including things added afterward.’ She was emphatic. ‘Which is the part that matters. An entry I write next month is automatically connected to everything I wrote last year, without my touching any of it.’

‘Which explicit linking cannot do.’

‘Which explicit linking cannot do without somebody going back and editing old material, which nobody does.’ Priya shrugged. ‘So a linked collection is most connected in the parts somebody was paying attention to.’

‘And least connected where it matters.’

‘And least connected in whatever they were rushing through, which is usually the busy period, which is usually when the interesting things happened.’

Raj made a point.

‘There is a cost and I want to state it.’

‘Say it.’

‘A link says something specific.’ He was precise. ‘This paper contradicts that one. This scene replaces that scene. A category does not say that. It says these two things concern the same subject.’

‘Which is less.’

‘Which is considerably less information per connection, and there are a hundred times as many of them.’ Raj shrugged. ‘Which is the trade and I think it is a good one for most purposes.’

‘Where would you want a link?’

‘When the relationship is the point rather than the subject.’ He counted. ‘This interview contradicts that one. That is not a shared subject. That is a specific claim about two specific things.’

‘Which I record in prose.’

‘Which you record in prose inside an entry, which is findable by searching, which is not the same as a link and is adequate.’ He was matter of fact. ‘I have perhaps forty of those. I have several thousand shared descriptions.’

Priya mentioned the suggestions.

‘There is something that generates connections I would not have made.’

‘Say it.’

‘When I create an entry, it proposes categories from my existing vocabulary based on what is in the content.’ She was precise. ‘And perhaps one time in five it proposes something I would not have applied.’

‘Which is wrong?’

‘Which is not wrong and is not what I was thinking about.’ Priya was definite. ‘Because I file something according to why I saved it, and the proposal is based on what is actually in it, and those diverge more than anybody expects.’

‘Give me a case.’

‘A paper I saved because of a delivery method, which also uses a particular imaging approach prominently, which I did not register because I was reading for something else.’ She shrugged. ‘Accepting that proposal took four seconds and it connected the paper to a subject I will write about in six months.’

‘So the proposal extends the fabric.’

‘The proposal extends it in directions I would not have, and it does so at the moment of creation rather than requiring a retrospective review.’

Raj asked about seeing the shape.

‘Can you look at the whole thing?’

‘The distribution across the vocabulary, which is a picture of what I actually cover.’ Priya pulled it up. ‘And which is the most honest description of my research that exists.’

‘Say what it shows.’

‘Which subjects have forty entries and which have four.’ She was matter of fact. ‘And the ones with four are either things I have not pursued or things I thought I had covered and have not.’

‘Which is a gap you can see.’

‘Which is a gap I would not otherwise see, because from inside every individual entry is fine.’ Priya was definite. ‘The absence is only visible in aggregate, and the aggregate is a picture rather than a list.’


## The subject Priya cannot trace

‘I want to describe where this fails,’ Priya said, ‘because we have spent an hour on things working.’

‘Say it.’

‘There is a subject I cannot trace and it is the most important one in my thesis.’

‘Which is?’

‘My own argument.’ She was precise. ‘Not a technique, not an organism, not a paper. The thing I am actually claiming.’

‘Say why that fails.’

‘Because an argument is not a subject. It is a relationship between subjects.’ Priya counted. ‘My claim is that one delivery approach produces a particular effect for a reason nobody has proposed, and there is no description that expresses that.’

‘So you cannot label it.’

‘I can label everything relevant to it, which gives me forty entries, and none of them is the argument.’ She shrugged. ‘The argument exists in one chapter and in my head.’

‘Which is a genuine limit.’

‘Which is a genuine limit and I want to be clear that I do not think it is a fault.’ Priya was definite. ‘A reference tells you what exists on a subject. It does not tell you what you think about it.’

‘Say what you do.’

‘I write the argument down, in prose, in one place, and I revise it.’ She was matter of fact. ‘Which is a document rather than a connection, and it is the only part of my work that is genuinely singular.’

Raj considered it.

‘Mine is identical and I would call it something different.’

‘Say yours.’

‘The film.’ He shrugged. ‘Which is a sequence of decisions about what matters and in what order, and nothing in my material expresses it.’

‘Because it is a judgement.’

‘Because it is a judgement rather than a subject, and my reference contains everything that informs it and cannot contain it.’ Raj was definite. ‘Which I think is correct rather than a shortcoming.’

‘Say why correct.’

‘Because if the material could produce the argument, the argument would not require me.’ He laughed. ‘Which is a slightly uncomfortable thought and I believe it.’

Priya nodded slowly.

‘That is the honest boundary and I am glad we have stated it,’ she said. ‘The reference makes me competent. It does not make me original.’


## Being shown something you did not ask for

‘Show me the suggestion thing properly,’ Raj said. ‘Because I have barely used it and you clearly rely on it.’

‘I use it constantly and it took about eight months to become useful.’ Priya opened an entry. ‘Watch what appears alongside this.’

‘Those are other entries.’

‘Those are other entries proposed on similarity, and the important thing is that I did not search for anything.’ She was precise. ‘I am reading one paper and it is offering me things that resemble it.’

‘Say why it took eight months.’

‘Because I trained it.’ Priya counted. ‘Every proposal I found useful, I marked up. Every one that was noise, I marked down. Perhaps four or five judgements a week.’

‘And it changed.’

‘It changed substantially and gradually, and I could not tell you when.’ She shrugged. ‘What I can tell you is that the proposals eighteen months ago were roughly random and the proposals now are correct about half the time.’

‘Half sounds low.’

‘Half is extraordinarily high for something proposing connections I did not make.’ Priya was emphatic. ‘A search returns what I asked for. This returns something I did not ask for, and one in two being worth reading is a remarkable rate.’

‘Say what the marking actually does.’

‘It persists.’ She was matter of fact. ‘My judgements are recorded and they affect what gets proposed and how things rank afterward. Which means the thing is learning my sense of what is relevant rather than a general one.’

‘Which is why it is not transferable.’

‘Which is why nobody else's would be useful to me and mine would be useless to you, because it is a model of my particular field and my particular interests.’ Priya paused. ‘Which is a strange kind of asset.’

Raj tried it on his own material.

‘That is interesting,’ he said after a moment.

‘Say what it gave you.’

‘I opened a research entry about denomination rules and it has proposed an interview transcript from a completely different production.’ He was reading. ‘Which is from a different country and a different subject and which discusses a protected designation.’

‘Which is the same structure.’

‘Which is the same regulatory structure applied to a different product, and I have never connected those two projects in any way.’ Raj was genuinely surprised. ‘I made that film four years ago.’

‘And it is proposing it.’

‘It is proposing it because the material resembles the material, and it has no idea that they are different projects because I never told it that mattered.’ He laughed. ‘Which is either a flaw or the entire point.’

‘It is the point.’

‘It is obviously the point and I am going to start marking these, because I have four productions worth of material that I think of as four separate things.’

‘And they are not.’

‘And they are apparently one body of material that happens to have been gathered on four occasions, which is a thought I have never had before this afternoon.’


## What it is worth professionally

‘I want to talk about what this is actually worth,’ Priya said, ‘because we have been describing a phenomenon and I have a practical case.’

‘Say it.’

‘My upgrade viva.’ She was matter of fact. ‘Which is an examination where two academics attempt to establish whether I understand my own field.’

‘Which is exactly the thing this addresses.’

‘Which is exactly the thing, and what I noticed preparing for it is that I was not learning anything.’ Priya counted. ‘I was reading what I had already written, which was organised, connected, and complete in ways I had forgotten.’

‘So the preparation was recall rather than study.’

‘The preparation was three weeks of reading my own reference on my own subject, and I went in knowing that any question they asked was somewhere in it.’ She was definite. ‘Which is a completely different psychological position from hoping.’

‘Did it help in the room?’

‘Twice, substantially.’ Priya was matter of fact. ‘Somebody asked about a methodological criticism I had not addressed in the document, and I had read about it eleven months earlier and written four lines that I could recall because I had reread them the previous week.’

‘Which without.’

‘Which without, I would have said I would look into it, which is an acceptable answer and a considerably weaker one.’

Raj said his version was commercial.

‘Mine is funding conversations,’ he said.

‘Say what happens.’

‘Somebody asks how deep my research goes, which is the question underneath every funding conversation about a documentary.’ He counted. ‘Because they are assessing whether I will actually deliver something or whether I have an interesting idea.’

‘And you can answer.’

‘I can answer with specifics, immediately, in the meeting.’ Raj was definite. ‘Somebody asked about the regulatory situation and I gave them the actual position, with the relevant date and the relevant body, because I searched it while they were speaking.’

‘Which changes the conversation.’

‘Which changes what kind of person they think they are talking to, and I am not exaggerating the effect.’ He shrugged. ‘There is a considerable difference between a filmmaker with a vision and a filmmaker who knows the subject.’

‘And you are the second.’

‘I am the second because I have eight months of accumulated material and four seconds of retrieval, and neither of those alone would do it.’

Priya raised the compounding point.

‘There is a property I want to name because it is the reason this keeps improving.’

‘Say it.’

‘Every entry I add makes every existing entry more useful.’ She was precise. ‘Which is not true of most things you accumulate.’

‘Say why.’

‘Because a new entry carrying a category connects to everything already carrying it.’ Priya counted. ‘So the value of the old material rises when I add something new, without my touching the old material.’

‘Which means the rate improves.’

‘Which means the thing gets more useful faster than it gets larger, which is the opposite of what happens to most collections.’ She was definite. ‘Most collections get harder to use as they grow. This one gets easier.’


## The part neither of them does

‘I want to be honest about something,’ Raj said, ‘because we have described this as emergent and there is a discipline underneath that we have not stated.’

‘Say it.’

‘Neither of us is casual about labelling.’

‘Which is true and I have been describing it as though it costs nothing.’

‘It costs four seconds per entry and it is the entire foundation.’ Raj was definite. ‘And I want to say plainly that somebody labelling inconsistently would get nothing from any of this.’

‘Say what inconsistent means.’

‘Using three different words for the same subject across two years.’ He counted. ‘Which is the standard failure and which produces three small groups instead of one large one.’

‘Which is worse than no labels at all.’

‘Which is arguably worse, because it looks like organisation and it fragments everything.’ Raj shrugged. ‘I have perhaps thirty categories and I have removed about twenty over four years.’

‘Mine is similar.’

‘Say what you removed.’

‘Things I created once and never applied again, mostly.’ Priya was matter of fact. ‘Which each seemed reasonable and which collectively were noise.’

‘And you check.’

‘I look at the small ones monthly, which takes about eleven minutes, and I either commit to something or remove it.’ She was definite. ‘Which is the only maintenance either of us does and it is what keeps the fabric coherent.’

Raj said something about titles.

‘There is a second discipline and it is smaller.’

‘Titles.’

‘Titles that say what the thing is rather than what it is called.’ He was matter of fact. ‘Because titles carry the most weight in retrieval, and a title that is a filename does nothing.’

‘Give me a case.’

‘An interview transcript titled with a person's name and nothing else.’ Raj counted. ‘Which tells me nothing about what is in it, which means it only ever surfaces if I search the person.’

‘And now?’

‘Now the title says the person and the subject, which takes four extra words and which makes it reachable from the subject as well as from the person.’ He shrugged. ‘Which is the smallest possible change and it doubled how findable my interviews are.’


## What happens when the subject moves

‘There is a problem I have and I want to know whether you do,’ Raj said.

‘Say it.’

‘My vocabulary describes a subject as I understood it eight months ago.’

‘Which has changed.’

‘Which has changed substantially, because I now understand that what I was calling economics is actually three things.’ He counted. ‘Land tenure, labour, and export regulation, which behave completely differently and which I had grouped.’

‘So the description is now too coarse.’

‘The description is too coarse and about eighty entries carry it, and splitting them means reading eighty entries.’ Raj shrugged. ‘Which I have not done and which I keep meaning to.’

‘Say what it costs you.’

‘Filtering on it gives me eighty things when I want twenty.’ He was matter of fact. ‘Which is not useless. It is one step short of useful.’

‘Mine is the same and I solved it badly.’

‘Say how.’

‘I added the three finer descriptions and applied them going forward without retrofitting.’ Priya was frank. ‘Which means my recent material is finely described and my older material is coarse.’

‘Which is a boundary in your own collection.’

‘Which is a date somewhere in the middle of my vocabulary where the descriptions change meaning, and I know roughly when it is, and nobody else would.’ She shrugged. ‘Which is fine for me and would be a genuine problem if anybody else used this.’

‘Say what you would advise.’

‘Retrofit the material you actually consult and leave the rest.’ Priya was definite. ‘Which is perhaps a quarter of it in my case, and which is an evening rather than a week.’

‘Rather than all of it.’

‘Rather than all of it, because the value of a finer description is proportional to how often you filter on it, and most of an archive is never filtered on.’ She counted. ‘So the eighty entries you are worried about are probably twenty entries that matter and sixty that do not.’

Raj thought about it.

‘That is more tractable than what I had been avoiding.’

‘It is the same advice everybody gives about migration and neither of us applied it to our own vocabulary,’ Priya said. ‘Which is apparently what happens when a problem develops slowly.’


## What a reference actually is

A wiki is not a technology. It is a condition: a body of knowledge dense enough and connected enough that a question produces an answer assembled from several places at once. The tooling that most people associate with the word is incidental, and the condition can arrive without anybody having set out to produce it.

Deliberate construction has a structural problem that defeats most attempts. A reference system with four entries is four entries; the value arrives only after substantial accumulation, which means months of effort during which the thing returns almost nothing. That ordering is why so many carefully designed personal wikis are abandoned during the first busy period. The tool was capable throughout and the sequence of costs and benefits was wrong.

The alternative route inverts it. Somebody who takes notes because they need the notes, structures them because structure helps them read their own material, and describes them because descriptions help them filter is doing work that pays on the day it is done. Nothing is being maintained on faith. Both practitioners here identified this as the entire difference between what happened to them and every deliberate attempt they had watched somebody abandon.

The threshold is identifiable and both placed it in the same region, somewhere between two and three hundred well-structured entries, with one suggesting a higher figure for material of more varied kinds since a subject needs covering from several directions before the effect appears. Below the threshold a query returns what somebody asked for. Above it, a query returns what they asked for and several things they did not, and the person reading the result learns something about their subject rather than locating a fact. Neither noticed the transition when it happened; both identified it months later.

Volume is necessary and insufficient, which is the qualification that matters most. Three hundred entries with inconsistent descriptions and no structure is a large pile. The discipline is the foundation and the volume is the multiplier, and somebody accumulating carelessly for two years has accumulated rather than built.

The connective mechanism is worth understanding precisely because it is not what people expect. An explicit link is a statement somebody made once, by hand, at a moment when they remembered, which means the connections in a manually linked collection are exactly as complete as their author's diligence and are least complete during whatever period was busiest. A shared description is a statement made once and applied to everything carrying it, including material added afterward, so an entry written next month connects to two years of existing work without anybody touching any of it.

The trade is real and both stated it. A link carries specific information: this contradicts that, this replaces that. A shared description carries considerably less per connection and produces a hundred times as many of them. Where a relationship rather than a subject is the point, both record it in prose, which is findable and is not the same thing, and both describe having perhaps forty such cases against several thousand shared descriptions.

Two mechanisms extend the fabric beyond what either person constructed. Suggestions at the moment of creation propose descriptions based on what is actually in an entry rather than on why somebody saved it, and those two diverge more than anybody expects. And contextual similarity proposes entries resembling whatever is open, which one practitioner trained over eight months with a few judgements a week and describes as correct roughly half the time, which is a remarkable rate for something proposing connections nobody made. During the conversation the other practitioner tried it and was offered material from a production he made four years earlier, on the grounds that a protected designation in one country structurally resembles one in another, which he had never connected because he thinks of his four productions as four separate things and they are apparently not.

The professional value takes different forms and rests on the same property. One prepared for an examination by reading her own reference on her own subject for three weeks, learning nothing new and recalling a great deal, and answered two questions in the room that she would otherwise have deferred. The other answers substantive questions during funding conversations by retrieving specifics while somebody is still speaking, which he describes as changing what sort of person the other party believes they are talking to.

The compounding property is what distinguishes this from most accumulations. A new entry carrying a description connects to everything already carrying it, which means adding material raises the value of existing material without anybody revisiting it. Most collections become harder to use as they grow. This one becomes easier, which is an unusual direction and the reason the effect accelerates rather than plateauing.
