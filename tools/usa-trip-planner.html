The most common planning mistake on a United States trip is underestimating distance. Two places that look adjacent on a national map can be a full day apart, and a route that reads as a gentle loop on screen frequently means six hours of driving on four of its seven days. The lines people forget
Sales tax is added at the till rather than shown on the shelf, and it varies by state and sometimes by city, so a budget built from displayed prices runs short. Hotel taxes and resort fees are frequently charged on top of the rate you booked. Tipping is expected in restaurants, bars, and for many services, and it is a real percentage of the food budget rather than a rounding error. One-way car rental fees can exceed the rental itself on a long drop. Build all four into the plan and the final total stops being a surprise.
